# OpenVamp desktop app

The desktop shell for Mac, Windows and Linux: the Perform screen from the design canvas, drawn with GPUI (`gpui` 0.2.2) and calling the `app` crate directly. It plays one song folder, or a setlist from a library folder, on the system's default audio output, at the song's sample rate.

```text
openvamp [SONG_DIR]
openvamp SETLIST_FILE
```

`SONG_DIR` is a song folder: `song.json` and the WAV or FLAC stems it lists; its `default` arrangement plays. `SETLIST_FILE` is a file ending in `.setlist.json` in the `Setlists` folder of a library folder (the folder's name may be in any case); the library folder is the one holding `Setlists`, and the setlist's song paths are relative to it. A relative path counts from the current folder, and `.` and `..` are resolved by name, as in `play-cli`. Without either, the screen offers **Open song…** (also Cmd-O on the Mac, Ctrl-O elsewhere) and **Open setlist…**. `--help` prints the usage; any other option, or a second argument, is named in the error and exits with code 2. A window that cannot open exits with code 1.

To try it without a song of your own, write the demo song (48 kHz, 8 positions, 48 s, one quiet chord stem) and open it:

```console
$ cargo run -p openvamp --example demo_song -- /tmp/demo-song
$ cargo run -p openvamp -- /tmp/demo-song
```

## The Perform screen

| Area | Shows and does |
|------|----------------|
| Header | **Open song…** and **Open setlist…**, or with a setlist its name and the current item's place (`2 of 5`), which opens another setlist; back to start, previous song and next song (with a setlist), play/pause, stop; the time in the arrangement, bar and beat, and the transport state (`Loading…` while play waits for the disk); **Fade** (shows the direction and progress); **Panic** (hold). Play/pause is an icon; under the clock the transport state shows only while it changes (Loading…, Stopping, Panic). The device chip on the right names the device, its outputs, buffer size and latency ("Interface · 8 out · 256 · 5.3 ms") with a dot: green, amber with underruns (their count joins the line), red with no device; it opens **Audio output**. |
| Setlist strip | With a setlist: a tile per item, in play order, with its number, title and what it plays (key, tempo, arrangement, start and end, fade-out; a click-only tile's tempo, meter and bars), marked played, current or upcoming; the next item is marked **Next**, or **Loading…** until its start is buffered. Between tiles, each item's transition as it plays (**Cue**, **Stay**, **Auto**, **X-fade**): an AutoLink or Crossfade into a song at another rate than the device plays as Cue next, since the device reopens between them, and its chip says so, as the setlist session reports it (`app::RateFallback`). A tile selects its item; the current item's own tile does nothing, so a stray tap never stops the song (back to start does that). A tile dragged along the strip and dropped between two others moves its item there while the setlist plays, with a line showing where it lands: the item playing plays on without a cut, and when the item after it changes, the new one is prepared and cued under the usual rules (another sample rate, a late link). The new order is saved to the setlist file, which the Library screen reads again; a file changed since the setlist was opened keeps the new order playing and says it was not saved. A move while a crossfade runs, or while an AutoLink hands over, is refused with a notice until it ends; dropping a tile on itself, on the **+**, or outside the strip moves nothing. An item that cannot play, or a song at another sample rate, shows why instead of what it plays. |
| Notes | When there is something to say: a device problem, the last problem in the `app` API's words, the song package's warnings, and with a setlist a crossfade under way (both items and its progress), every reason an item cannot play, each song at another sample rate than the device, the setlist's last problem (an item that could not be prepared, such as a start or end its song no longer has; a link that was late; a transition the audio thread refused), a previous or next that had nowhere to go (until the setlist moves on), why the library's songs could not be listed (items then show their folder names), each link that plays as Cue next because of another rate, the device held for an item at a rate it refuses, and why the song's key, tempo and meter are missing when its `song.json` cannot be read (it is read again 5 seconds later while the song shows, and whenever a song or setlist opens; a read the drive does not answer within 5 seconds counts as failed, and a re-read that fails keeps the key, tempo and meter read before). |
| Now and Next | The section playing and the time left in it; what plays next: the queued jump or loop and when it executes, the current section again under the infinite loop, the next section, or the end of the song. A queued jump can be cancelled here. Each card carries its section's icon in the section colour and counts bars: "11 bars left", "Plays in 11 bars". |
| Song map | Every arrangement position in play order, sized by length and colored by section family; the playhead; the queued jump's target (dashed) and a marker where it executes, with the time until then. A section without a number that plays more than once is numbered by its turn (Chorus 1 and Chorus 2, C1 and C2); a Tag is the exception and stays Tag each time. A bar ruler under the map numbers the bars in play order. The track under the blocks shows the song's waveform: one bar per musical bar inside each position's block, as tall as that bar's level in the song's overview (18 to 96 %), in its section's color, opaque once played and faded ahead of the playhead; a section that repeats or plays out of order shows its own audio each time. Until the waveform is read, or when it cannot be, the track shows the sections' colors with the played part lit. |
| Section controls | **Jump at**: section end or next bar; **Loop section** (repeat the current section once more; press again to drop it); **Infinite**. |
| Pads | One per arrangement position, marked **Now** and **Next**, with the section's icon, its abbreviation (V1, PC, C1), its name and bar count; they share one row when they fit and wrap otherwise. A pad queues a jump to its position; the queued pad, pressed again, cancels it. |
| Song header | The song's title, then its key, tempo and meter (read from its `song.json` in the background, so reading them never holds up the screen; until the file is read the header shows without them; a click tile shows its own tempo and meter), and its arrangement as a dropdown-style chip, which does not switch arrangements yet. |
| Mixer | At the bottom, when the window leaves room for it: the Mixer screen's bus strips and master, with faders and mutes, and **Buses** · **Stems** (Stems opens the Mixer screen on its stems layer). Meter slots stay empty until the engine reports levels. |

The song map's waveform is read from the song's `waveforms/` cache whatever the transport does; reading it decodes no stem. When the cache is missing or stale, the waveform is built from the song's stems on a background thread only while the transport is stopped or paused, so a song that plays never shares its drive with a build: until then the track shows the sections' colors. Play cancels a build under way, which leaves no cache behind, and the next stop or pause starts it again. Opening another song, or a setlist moving on to its next item, cancels the build for the song before. The audio thread does nothing for it. The Song editor (below) builds its waveform whatever plays.

There is no status bar: each control shows its key in a tooltip ("Play · Space", "Back to start · B", "Queue Verse 1 · 2"), and the keys are listed under **Keys** below.

**Panic** fires only after the button, or the X key, is held for one second; the fill shows the hold. A click or a tap never fires it, and neither does a hold whose release the window cannot see: when the window loses focus during the hold (Cmd-Tab, a system dialog, the folder picker), the hold ends without panic.

The session is polled about 30 times a second on a timer that keeps running while the window is hidden, since a waiting play starts only through polling.

**Open setlist…** asks for a setlist file. The setlist is read and checked against the library's songs before anything stops; a setlist with no item that can play leaves what plays playing and says why for each item. Otherwise it opens like a song (below), at its first playable item, stopped at its start, on a device opened at the first playable song's rate; each song item is named by its title from the library, or by its folder name when the library does not have it. All the transport controls act on the current item; previous and next skip the items that cannot play. A setlist and a song replace each other the same way a song replaces a song.

**Open song…** first checks the new folder and loads it without the audio device, on a background thread, while the current song keeps playing and every control, Panic included, keeps answering, however long a slow, sleeping or unplugged drive takes; if another song or setlist is picked meanwhile, only the newest pick opens. Then it asks the device, without opening it, whether it can play the song: that the device is there and takes the song's sample rate, the buffer size and stereo. A folder that does not open, a device that cannot play the song, or a folder picker that fails, leaves the current song playing (or, with its device unplugged, loaded and waiting for it to come back) and shows why under the header until dismissed. A device that cannot tell without opening, such as a busy one, goes ahead. Otherwise the current song stops with its ramp and closes the device, and only then does the new song open it: on macOS, opening a stream sets the device's sample rate, which would cut off a song still playing on it, and some interfaces take one stream at a time. If opening still fails (the stream cannot be built or started), the previous song is loaded again (in the background too), stopped at the start, and both reasons show. A setlist opens the same way, and loads each next song in the background while the current one plays: a next song still loading when it is due follows on as soon as it has loaded, and its tile shows **Loading…** until then.

Quitting or closing the window while a song plays or waits to start (`Loading…`) asks first (and while a song is being saved, as the Library section says): **Stop and quit** or **Keep playing**, so that neither Space nor Escape ends the show. On the Mac, Return picks Stop and quit, and Escape (or Space, on the focused button) keeps playing. On Windows, Keep playing comes first as the default: Enter, Space and Escape keep playing. On Linux, gpui draws the question inside the window and it is answered with a click; while it is open, the screen's controls do nothing. Stop, the transport control, stays immediate. With nothing playing, both quit at once. The app sets no menus of its own. Quitting from the Dock, or when the system logs out, cannot be asked about (gpui 0.2.2 cannot cancel it), so the song just stops with its ramp.

Closing a song (opening another one, closing the window, quitting) stops it with the usual short ramp before the device closes. If the player refuses Stop because its command queue is full, Stop is sent again every 5 ms for up to 500 ms. A refusal, or a ramp that has not ended by then, is reported on stderr, and on screen when another song replaced it.

## The Library & setlists screen

The side navigation switches between **Perform**, **Library**, **Mixer** and, at the bottom, **Audio output** (the design canvas's other screens are not built yet, so they are not shown). The Library screen shows one library folder: the one picked with **Open library…**, which stays, or, until one is picked, the library of the setlist on the Perform screen, which it follows when a setlist from another library is opened there. Renaming the setlist that is on Perform, or the first change that moves a hand-named file, is followed there: it opens again from its new file and stays marked.

| Area | Shows and does |
|------|----------------|
| Setlists | Every setlist of the library's `Setlists` folder in its stored order, with its item count and whether it is open on Perform; files that cannot be read, with the reason and a **Remove** of their own (two presses, as below), since a broken file can still be taken out of the list. A setlist is dragged by its grip, or anywhere on its row, and dropped between two others: a line shows where it lands, and dropping it on itself or outside the list moves nothing. The arrows beside it move it up or down too. Either way the order is kept in `Setlists/order.json` and lasts. A stored order that cannot be used says why, and the setlists are then listed by name. **New** creates an empty setlist ("New setlist", numbered when taken) and starts naming it; with an order stored it goes at the end, and a duplicate right after its original. The library folder and **Open library…** at the bottom. |
| Editor | The selected setlist's name (the pencil renames it, the copy button duplicates it as "NAME copy" and starts naming the copy, **Remove** takes it out of the list: the first press arms it and reads "Click again to remove NAME", the second moves its file to the system trash, where it can be restored, and puts the next setlist, or the one before, in the editor; any other action disarms it, as do leaving the Library screen and any control elsewhere, and the setlist open on Perform cannot be removed, whatever case its file name was opened in), its item count and length, and **Perform**, which opens it on the Perform screen as Open setlist… does. One row per item: grip, move up and down, number, title and what it plays, arrangement, transition out, length, remove. An item is dragged by its grip, or anywhere on its row, and dropped between two others, as a setlist is: a line shows where it lands, the setlist saves at once, and dropping it on itself or outside the list changes nothing. A drag disarms **Remove**. Clicking an item shows its controls: for a song its arrangement (choosing another plays it in full again), start and end positions by section (start never after end), the five-second fade-out and the transition; for a click-only tile its tempo, meter, bar count (down from one bar plays until you move on) and transition. **Add song** goes to the song search, **Add click tile** adds 120 BPM in 4/4. Every rule the setlist breaks shows under its item, in the `domain` crate's words. |
| Songs | Every song folder of the library (`library::Library::list_songs`) by title, with key, tempo, length and stem count, and folders that cannot be read; the search keeps the songs whose title holds the text, whose key is it, or whose tempo starts with it. **Import stems** adds a song to the library from a folder or ZIP of stems (below). The edit link (the waveform icon) opens the song in the Song editor to edit its sections and arrangements (below). **+** adds a song at the end of the setlist, playing its Default arrangement in full; with no setlist in the editor it does nothing. |

Every change is saved at once through the `library` crate, which writes the setlist file atomically and never overwrites another setlist; a name another setlist already uses is refused and says so. When a setlist is saved but its place in the stored order cannot be written, a warning (not a failure) says so, and the setlist is listed after the ordered ones until it is moved. A setlist file named by hand is moved to the file its name maps to on its first change, so it never ends up in two files. The Perform screen plays a setlist as it was when it was opened: changes to it play once it is opened again, which the editor says while it is open there.

### Importing stems

**Import stems** (F1) asks for a folder or a `.zip` file of stems in WAV, FLAC, MP3 or M4A; a folder or ZIP dropped on the button does the same (the first one, when several are dropped). The source is read on a background thread, so a slow or sleeping drive never holds up the screen or Panic, and the button reads "Reading NAME…" meanwhile; if another source is picked before it is read, only the newest opens. A source with no audio file, a file that is neither a folder nor a ZIP, or a ZIP that cannot be read says why on the Library screen. What the import takes from a source, what it copies and what it transcodes to FLAC is in the `library` crate's `import` docs.

The **Song editor** then shows in place of the Library screen, after the design canvas's Song editor. An import has one Intro section, so its section markers and arrangement panels are not shown: they are edited once the song is saved, from its edit link (below).

| Area | Shows and does |
|------|----------------|
| Header | **Library** goes back to the Library and drops the import, as **Discard** does. **Title** (the folder's or ZIP's name to start with), **Key** (optional), **Tempo** (120 BPM to start; a point or a comma before decimals) and **Time** (4/4 to start, typed as `6/8`) are typed into as the setlist's name is; Escape puts back what the field held. **Tempo or meter change** adds a change eight bars after the last one, at the tempo in force there. **Discard** drops the song, or cancels the save under way. **Save song** saves it; it is off while anything listed under the header blocks the song, and while it saves it reads "Saving… 42%" and fills with the progress. |
| Notes | Under the header, in red, everything that keeps the song from being saved, in the `domain` and `library` crates' words: a tempo or time signature that is no number, a tempo out of range, a change at bar 1 or past the end of the song, stems at another sample rate than the song (each file named), files the import refuses, and why the last save failed (Save song can be pressed again). In amber, what does not block it: stem lengths that differ by more than a second. |
| Tempo and meter | The song's tempo and meter at bar 1, then each change: the bar it starts (counted from 1; bars restart at a time-signature change), its tempo and its time signature, either left empty to keep it, and a button that removes it. |
| Stems | "16 · 48 kHz · FLAC": the stem count, the song's sample rate and the format the stems are stored in (WAV, FLAC, or "WAV + FLAC" when mixed), then the source. Each stem's instrument icon in its bus's color, its file, **AUTO** while its part comes from its file name (`EG 1.wav` is an electric guitar, on Guitars), and its bus button, whose hint names the part. The bus button opens the part menu under the row: every part, under its bus's heading; choosing one gives the stem that part, moves it to that part's bus and drops AUTO. As many stems show as the window has room for, and **Show N more** shows the rest. Files the import refuses are listed under **Cannot import** with the reason; while there is one, the song cannot be saved. |

**Save song** writes the song on a background thread into the library folder as `TITLE.song` (numbered when taken), building it in a hidden folder that is renamed into place only once complete, while the timer draws its progress. The Library screen then shows again with the song list read again: the new song is marked (until the next control), the search is cleared when it would hide it, and the list scrolls to it. **Discard** during the save cancels it within about a second of audio and leaves nothing behind; the editor reads "Cancelling…" until it has stopped, and a save that had already finished is kept, listed and said so. Opening another library while a song saves cancels the save too, and waits (5 seconds at most) until it has stopped.

Quitting or closing the window while a song is being saved asks first, whether or not a song plays: **Quit** or **Keep saving**, in the order the playing question uses, so neither Space nor Escape quits. **Quit** stops the song that plays at once and cancels the save; the app quits, or the window closes, only once the save has stopped and removed its hidden folder (10 seconds at most, should the drive hang). A quit that cannot be asked about (the Dock, logging out) waits in the same way, 5 seconds at most, before the app ends. A hidden folder an import still leaves behind (the app crashed or was killed) is removed by a later import, once nothing in it has changed for an hour (the `library` crate's `import` docs).

### Editing a song

A song row's edit link (F2) reads the song on a background thread (the Import stems button reads "Opening TITLE…" meanwhile; a song that cannot be read says why on the Library screen) and opens it in the **Song editor**, after the design canvas's Song editor:

| Area | Shows and does |
|------|----------------|
| Header | As for an import: **Title**, **Key**, **Tempo**, **Time** and **Tempo or meter change** edit the song; its changes show under the inspector. A song whose tempo or meter changes do not start bars (written by another tool) shows them read-only, says so in amber, and saves them as they are. **Library** and **Discard** go back to the Library; with edits not saved they ask first (below). **Save song** is off while a field reads as no value or a change does not fit in the song. |
| Section markers | A bar ruler labelled every 8 bars; the marker strip, one block per section as wide as its bars, in its section's color, with its abbreviation and the bar it starts ("Bar 9", or "Bar 9.3" on its third beat); and the waveform, one bar per musical bar, as tall as that bar's level in the song's overview (16 to 94 %), in its section's color. Until the overview is read the bars are flat and say how far it is. A click on the ruler or the waveform places the cursor (the "playhead"), snapped; **Add marker at playhead** splits the section there, in every arrangement. **Snap to bar** snaps to the nearest bar; off, to the nearest beat. A marker is dragged by its block or by its line on the waveform: the place it lands shows while it moves, and it snaps there when released. Zoom out and in change how many bars show (4 at the closest), around the cursor; the wheel or the trackpad scrolls through the song. Clicking a block selects its section. |
| Inspector | The selected section: **Type** (a menu of the built-in types), **Number** (typed; empty for none), **Starts at**, **Length**, **Infinite loop by default** for the arrangement shown (a section it does not play cannot loop there), and **Delete marker** (the first marker starts the song and stays; a marker whose section is all an arrangement plays stays too). |
| Arrangement | A tab per arrangement and **New**, which adds one playing every section once and starts naming it; the pencil renames the arrangement shown and the bin deletes it, except Default. An arrangement a setlist of the library plays cannot be deleted: the note names the setlists. Its sections as chips, each with its grip, abbreviation, bars and ×; a chip is dragged between two others to move it, as a setlist's items are. **Add a section to the end**: one button per section. The summary: "11 sections · 136 bars · 5:14". |
| Stems | As for an import, without the source line: the part menu changes a stem's part. |

Every edit goes through the `domain` crate's song editor: one it refuses (a marker where one starts, a move past a neighbour, an arrangement left with no sections, a name another arrangement has) changes nothing and shows under the header in its words, until the next edit that applies. Markers are sample positions, so a tempo or meter edit keeps each one on its audio and redraws the bars from the same overview.

The waveform comes from the song's `waveforms/` cache, or is built from its stems on a background thread when the cache is missing or stale (the `library` crate's `waveform` docs); closing the editor cancels the build. **Save song** rewrites the song's `song.json` on a background thread (`library::Library::save_song`), and an arrangement deleted also leaves its `mix.json`; the Library then shows again with the songs read anew and the song marked. A song open on Perform keeps playing as it was opened. A save that fails keeps the editor open with the reason.

Leaving the editor with edits not saved — **Discard**, **Library**, opening another library, quitting or closing the window — asks first: **Discard changes** or **Keep editing**, in the order the playing question uses, so neither Space nor Escape drops them.

The setlist's name, the song search and the Song editor's fields are typed with the keyboard while they have the focus: Enter keeps the text, Escape leaves it (a rename is not made, the search is cleared, a Song editor field gets back what it held; clicking another control keeps what a Song editor field holds), Backspace deletes the last character, Cmd-V (Ctrl-V elsewhere) pastes. Every other key types, Space, `x` and the digits too, so the transport keys and the panic key do nothing while typing; Cmd/Ctrl-O and Cmd/Ctrl-Q still work. There is no caret to move and no input-method composition, since gpui 0.2.2 has no text field of its own.

## The Mixer screen

The Mixer mixes the song on Perform (a setlist's current item) as it plays (F7). **Stems** has a strip per stem: its name, a bus chip, a fader, its level in dB and **M** and **S**. The bus chip shows its choices: **Default** puts the stem back on the bus the rules table gives it, any other bus moves it there. **Buses** has a strip per bus with the output it plays on in the routing in use ("Out 1", "Out 7/8"), a fader, its level and **M**, then the master. A fader drags smoothly and sends its level as it moves; a double click puts it back at 0 dB; at the bottom it reads `-inf`, at the top +12 dB, with 0 dB a little above the middle. A stem that plays nothing (muted, its bus muted, or another stem soloed; solo never silences click or guide) is drawn dimmed.

**Save mix to arrangement** writes the stems' levels, mutes, solos and buses to the song's `mix.json` for the arrangement playing; it is offered only when the mix differs from what that file holds, and a folder that refuses the file says why. **Reset to song mix** puts every stem back at 0 dB, unmuted, unsoloed and on its own bus, as the song plays with no saved mix; saving then forgets the arrangement's mix. Bus and master levels are global: they carry from song to song and are saved to the app's settings folder half a second after the last change (and before a song closes), so the next session opens with them. There are no meters: the engine does not report levels yet.

## The Audio output screen

**Audio output** (F8) shows the output device in use, which the device button changes (every device with its outputs, such as "Interface · 8 outputs"; another device opens the song again on it, stopped), the rate it runs at, and the buffer size, 64 to 1024 frames, with the latency it adds; a new size applies at once. A 2-output device has a **Split mode** switch: click and guide on the left, the rest summed to mono on the right. The routing matrix has a row per bus and a column per output; a dot marks where each bus plays. A click on an output of a bus on one output makes them a stereo pair; a click on one side of a pair leaves the bus on the other side alone; a click on a third output starts over, with the bus on that output alone; the only output of a bus stays, since a bus always plays somewhere. A click while split mode is on turns it off. **Preset** offers the presets for 1, 2 (split mode), 3, 4, 6 and 8 outputs that fit the device, and **Reset** goes back to the device's own. Every change applies to the song playing at once and is saved for the device in the app's settings folder; with nothing playing, the device the next song opens on is edited.

Every song and setlist opens on the device chosen (else the system's default) with the routing saved for it, fitted to its outputs, or the preset for its outputs (a 2-output device starts in split mode), with the global bus levels and the buffer size chosen. Settings files that cannot be used, a routing refitted to a device that came back with fewer outputs, and a device with too few outputs show as notices; with no settings folder, the settings last until the app closes, and the screen says so. The design canvas's MIDI settings are not built (F9).

## Keys

The same single keys as `play-cli`; letters work in either case, and a key held with Ctrl, Alt or Cmd does nothing (except Open and Quit). A key held down acts once: its repeats are ignored until it is released, on every platform (on X11, gpui reports each repeat as a new press, so the screen tracks the keys that are down itself). On the Mac, pressing Cmd counts every key as up, since macOS never reports a key released while Cmd is held; elsewhere the Super or Windows key changes nothing.

| Key | Does |
|-----|------|
| Space | Play or pause. |
| `s` | Stop and return to the start. |
| `b` | Back to the start. |
| `1`–`9` | Queue a jump to arrangement position 1–9 (again on the queued one: cancel). |
| `n` | Jump timing: section end or next bar. |
| `l` | Loop section, or drop the queued loop. |
| `i` | Infinite loop on or off. |
| `f` | Fade the track stems out, or back in. |
| `c`, Escape | Cancel the queued jump or loop. |
| `,` / `.` | With a setlist: previous / next song (as in `play-cli`). |
| `x` (hold 1 s) | Panic: fade everything out in 0.5 s and stop. |
| Cmd-O / Ctrl-O | Open a song folder. |
| Cmd-Q / Ctrl-Q | Quit; while a song plays, only after confirming. A plain `q` does nothing, unlike `play-cli`, so a stray key on stage cannot end the show. |

## Building

On macOS, GPUI's `runtime_shaders` feature compiles the Metal shaders when the app starts, so the build needs no Metal Toolchain (Xcode 26 ships it as a separate download). The screens use the design canvas's fonts, which the app carries in `assets/fonts` and loads at startup: Barlow for the interface, Barlow Semi Condensed for strip, pad and stem labels, and JetBrains Mono for times, levels and counts. All three are under the SIL Open Font License 1.1; each license file sits beside its fonts (`Barlow-OFL.txt`, `BarlowSemiCondensed-OFL.txt`, `JetBrainsMono-OFL.txt`).

The view-model (`src/view.rs`, `src/strip.rs`, `src/catalog.rs`, `src/action.rs`, `src/hold.rs`, `src/keys.rs`, `src/song.rs`, `src/show.rs`, `src/ending.rs`, `src/tick.rs`, and for the Library screen `src/library_page.rs`, `src/editor.rs`, `src/shelf.rs`, `src/line_edit.rs` and the Song editor's `src/song_import.rs`, `src/song_header.rs`, `src/song_edit.rs` and `src/song_sections.rs`) holds every decision the screens make and is tested without a window (`cargo test -p openvamp`); the GPUI code (`src/screen.rs`, `src/ui.rs`, `src/library_ui.rs`, `src/song_editor_ui.rs`, `src/song_sections_ui.rs` and the others ending in `_ui.rs`) only draws it and dispatches, and has no automated test beyond the pickers' options.
